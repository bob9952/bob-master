Master Thesis Nikola Subotic
**“Solving the One-Dimensional Bin Packing Problem Using a Genetic Algorithm”**
 
### Significance of the Topic and Research Area
 
The Bin Packing Problem (BPP) is one of the best-known problems in discrete and combinatorial optimisation and belongs to the class of NP-hard problems. In its basic one-dimensional form, known as the One-Dimensional Bin Packing Problem, the objective is to arrange a set of objects of different sizes into the smallest possible number of bins with a fixed capacity, while ensuring that the total size of the objects placed in each bin does not exceed its capacity.
 
Bin packing problems have applications in areas such as logistics, industrial packaging, resource allocation in computer systems, and memory allocation. Due to their computational complexity, finding exact solutions for larger instances is often impractical. Therefore, heuristic and metaheuristic algorithms are commonly used to quickly obtain high-quality approximate solutions.
 
### Specific Objective of the Thesis
 
This thesis will apply a genetic algorithm as a representative of metaheuristic methods for solving the one-dimensional bin packing problem. The obtained solutions will be compared with the results of classical heuristic approaches, such as First-Fit, Best-Fit, Next-Fit, and Max-Rest, including their sorted variants, First-Fit Decreasing and Next-Fit Decreasing.
 
The objective of the thesis is to examine the potential of genetic algorithms for solving the one-dimensional bin packing problem through comparison with classical heuristic methods. The analysis will include criteria such as the number of bins used, the quality of the obtained solutions, and the execution time of the algorithms.
